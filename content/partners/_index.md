The **Open Force Field Consortium** is a pre-competitive, industry-funded collaboration building more accurate small molecule force fields for computational drug discovery. Consortium members don't just fund the work — they shape it, providing scientific direction through our Advisory Board and Governing Board.

{{< br >}}

## Why join?

Industry partners bring real-world expertise in force field applications that directly improves our science. In return, partners get:

- Direct input on scientific priorities and project roadmaps through the Consortium Advisory Board
- Early access to new force fields and software
- Dedicated collaboration opportunities with leading academic groups
- Recognition as a supporting partner

{{< br >}}

## Get in touch

Interested in joining or learning more? There are a few ways to get started:

1. Watch the [keynote from our latest annual meeting](https://youtu.be/vbv0wZf-iVI?si=EMRk-i2ifZvI96EB) and download the [slide deck](https://zenodo.org/records/13343917/files/2024%20OpenFF%20Keynote%20(public).pdf?download=1) for an overview of our work.
2. Email us at <a href="mailto:info@openforcefield.org">info@openforcefield.org</a> to schedule a short introductory call — we'll walk through membership levels and ways to contribute.
3. Request a formal presentation for your team by emailing <a href="mailto:info@openforcefield.org">info@openforcefield.org</a>.

{{< br >}}

## Collaborate with us

Beyond the Consortium, we welcome scientific and software contributions:

**Science** — We have spin-off projects where external collaborators can make high-impact contributions, and we actively support relevant grant applications. Contact <info@openforcefield.org> to discuss ideas.

**Software** — Contributions to our open-source tools are always welcome. See our [contributing guidelines](https://open-forcefield-toolkit.readthedocs.io/en/latest/users/developing.html) to get started, or open an issue on our [GitHub tracker](http://github.com/openforcefield/openforcefield/issues).

**Data** — We are always seeking high-quality datasets for force field optimization and benchmarking. Reach out if you have molecular sets, binding free energies, condensed phase properties, or crystal structures to share.
